Gaps Identified in Residential Societies

Shared spaces serve differing interests (Abhinav's point, transcribed as "interest defer"), creating governance and coordination problems but also collective-interest opportunities.

Example: a builder's lawn ends up used by kids as a football ground

Information exists in societies but stays fragmented: residents miss events, power cuts or water issues unless prompted repeatedly.

Businesses can reach concentrated demand but lack community-level distribution inside societies, a chance to create new markets.

Households act as individual economic units while housing becomes more concentrated, pointing to a potential collective economy.



Core Hypothesis and Approach

Core hypothesis: investigate the gap between what collective infra makes possible and what individual behavior currently captures.

Value can be economic, psychological, systems or other; this closes the first, most abstract phase of the case study.

Method: start vaguely and explore every angle, but converge on a one-line objective before going deep.
