### Field Research Recap

- Group conducted ~23 interviews across Tier 1 cities (Gurgaon, Pune, Bangalore)
- Stakeholders covered: security guards, maids, cooks, car cleaners, dog walkers, gig workers, residents, small vendors
- Methods varied: in-person, phone, WhatsApp, overheard lunch conversations (Sonam used WhisperFlow)
- Anil’s experience in Trishul: turned away by supervisor, told to get head office permission first

### Key Field Findings

- Gig workers commonly hold double shifts or multiple jobs to supplement low wages (~₹15-20k/month)
  - Security guards forced to stand throughout shifts; residents object to them sitting
  - Workers lack basic amenities: water, rest space, shelter from extreme weather
- Female domestic workers face financial control issues
  - Wages often go into husband’s account; women have limited direct access
  - One maid’s gold savings (via Tanishq scheme) were sold by her husband
- Security shift handovers create a ~30-minute window with no guards at the gate
- Small vendors (women-run home enterprises) face intense competition and repeat-visit fatigue in societies
- Residents largely transactional with neighbors; social ties form mainly through kids or long-term residency

### Primary vs. Secondary Research Synthesis

- Combined 21 primary interviews with Reddit, Google Maps, and news scraping
- Common themes across both:
  - Gate friction for delivery and service workers
  - Worker insecurity and poor conditions
  - Committee workload and trust deficit
  - Maintenance and water repair costs (large, lumpy, disputed)
- Reddit-only themes (not in interviews): rules on guests, pets, tenant access
- Interview-only themes (not online): worker welfare, neighbor isolation
  - Workers don’t post on Reddit; residents who complain online skew young and conflict-prone

### Deepak’s Analytical Framework

- Structured Claude prompt for unbiased analysis:
  - Act as independent research analyst
  - Extract evidence-backed stakeholder motivations, unmet needs, workarounds, conflicts, incentive misalignments
  - Distinguish evidence from interpretation; quantify recurrence
  - Actively challenge confirmation bias, sampling bias, overrepresentation of vocal stakeholders
  - “Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence”
- Used Claude Sonnet (Opus 5.5 recommended for better output with strict guardrails)
- Output: structured evidence table with counterintuitive insights and research gaps

### Opportunity Space Scoring

Group voted on 4 dimensions: Reach, User Impact, Frequency, Economic Value

- Gate as shared checkpoint for repeat visitors: high reach, high frequency, low economic value
  - Pre-approval and ID-based entry already partially solved by MyGate
- Accountability gap (complaints move to WhatsApp, not acted on in apps): moderate across all dimensions
- Society running costs (water, repairs, unpaid arrears): high reach and user impact, low frequency
- Worker welfare and site conditions: high reach and user impact, low economic value
  - Deepak’s challenge: demand-supply imbalance means no clear business ROI for vendors or societies
- Internal labor market for household help (one-way ratings, churn): high across all dimensions, including economic value

### Opportunity Spaces Identified

- Hyperlocal distribution: dark store within large society for groceries and essentials
- Mobility / carpooling: middle layer for residents commuting to same office hubs
  - Caveat: Uber Pool failed in India; off-app drift once people know each other
- Childcare and elderly care: subscription-based collective care within society
- Household management: managed services model (co-working analogy: pay per seat, third party handles everything)
- Specialized service marketplace: STP/WTP operators, fire safety, pool maintenance
- Sustainability: solar, rainwater harvesting, energy efficiency via shared infra
- **Shareability marketplace** (highest votes): platform for residents to share/rent items and services within society
  - Equipments and gadgets (cameras, PS5, baby gear)
  - Cars and parking spaces
  - Food dishes (home-cooked, on-demand)
  - Coaches, mentors, tutors, babysitters, sports trainers
  - Jobs and referrals (e.g. office nearby, walk-to-work leads)
  - Hobby groups and workshops

### Deepak’s Key Guidance

- Don’t jump to solutions: still in observatory phase, problem statement not yet clear
- Zoom out, not in: avoid locking onto a persona or solution prematurely
- Systems thinking: every node is connected; solving for one stakeholder creates friction for another
  - Example: better security = harder visitor access; larger society = more amenities but more walking
- Worker exploitation persists because everyone benefits from the status quo
  - Vendors save on labor costs; residents get cheap services; workers’ cost of raising voice is too high
- Root cause of gate friction is distrust, not technology; same person writing in a register vs. an app
- Distrust question left open: information asymmetry or conflict of interest?
- AI guardrails: always keep raw data word-for-word; AI loses context and hallucinates when paraphrasing user conversations
