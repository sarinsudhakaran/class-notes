# Case Study Overview

- Focus: India’s growing apartment ecosystem as a complex economic and social system
- Goal: Discover unmet needs and emerging opportunities, not improve existing apps (MyGate, NoBrokerhood, etc.)
- Framing: Understand the ecosystem from first principles before deciding what to build

# Stakeholder Mapping

- Key actor groups identified:
  - Residents: renters, homeowners, landlords, bachelors, families, dog owners
  - Workers: security guards (some on 24-hour shifts), maids, cooks, drivers
  - Delivery partners: Zepto, Blinkit, Swiggy, etc.
  - Local business owners: kirana shops disrupted by quick commerce
  - Society management and admin
- Security guards highlighted as underserved: exposed to all weather, unpaid for informal duties (e.g., delivering orders after 11 PM)

# Problem Space Discussion

- Isolation in large societies: most residents know fewer than 5-7 neighbors despite living in 1,000+ family complexes
- Behavioral shift: delivery coordination has smoothed, but other friction points remain hidden
- Security asymmetry (Vikas): strict entry controls for visitors, but internal theft/loss goes unresolved
- Existing apps (MyGate, NoBrokerhood) cover most resident-facing problems; unmet needs lie deeper
- Sohan: focus on improving sub-workflows within existing behaviors, not creating new categories
- Sonam: distributed procurement across a society could be aggregated into collective purchasing

# First Principles Thinking

- Core lesson: always dissect problem statements word by word before jumping to solutions
- 5-layer analysis framework (Deepak):
  1. Events: what is observable
  2. Patterns: what trends emerge over time
  3. Behavioral psychology: what behaviors arise from those patterns
  4. Human psychology / neuroscience: ego, desire, attention
  5. Consciousness: the root driver of all human movement
- “Why” is the deepest layer; “what” and “how” follow from it
- BA vs. PM distinction: BA drills into given requirements; PM challenges them; PM finds the gap independently

# Product Thinking: 7 Sins Framework

- Every great consumer product maps to one or more of the 7 sins: pride, greed, lust, envy, gluttony, wrath, sloth
- Instagram uses all 7; Duolingo invests heavily in psychologists to exploit the attention loop
- Discovery feed exists to trigger human curiosity, the 5th-layer driver
- FOMO is a form of lust: desire for something you fear missing, amplified by social comparison
- Funnel design (not UI): how a product moves users from one interaction to the next, keeping them scrolling

# Mental Models Introduced

- First principles: break any concept into absolute basic truths, layer by layer, until you can rebuild it
  - Apple tree example: same tree, different soil/climate, different outcome; led to hydroponics
- Chesterton’s Fence: never remove or change a friction until you understand why it exists
  - Example: late-night delivery ban protects high-net-worth residents; removing it introduces new risk
- Detective mindset: observe every actor’s perspective without bias or verdict; surface all aspects of the system

# Discovery Process and Funnel

- Reverse pyramid / funnel approach:
  - Start wide (abstract case study) and cut down iteratively to one specific problem
  - One user type → eliminate to one core problem → one primary solution
- Secondary research first: scope cities, building types, average rents, society sizes
  - Tier 1 cities (Pune, Hyderabad, Gurgaon, Mumbai) have mature apartment behavior
  - Tier 2 cities have emerging behavior; different signals, harder to access primary data
- Primary research only after scoping: talk to 10-12 people each across the group to reach ~100 interviews
- Questionnaire to be built together on the call; Google Forms discouraged, open conversations preferred
